Title: Attention‑Gated 3‑D UNet for Brain Tumour Segmentation
Authors: Li et al.

ABSTRACT
Accurate tumour segmentation in MRI is critical for personalised treatment planning.
We propose an attention‑gated 3‑D UNet that balances parameter efficiency with volumetric
context capture.  Evaluated on the BraTS 2020 dataset, the model achieves an average Dice
of 0.89, outperforming prior 2‑D approaches while reducing inference latency by 27 %.

INTRODUCTION
Deep learning has revolutionised medical imaging, yet volumetric consistency across slices
remains challenging.  Prior 2‑D UNets suffer from discontinuities because they process each
slice independently.  Volumetric 3‑D networks deliver superior coherence but often carry a
hefty parameter count.  Li et al. [3] highlighted three obstacles: limited GPU memory,
class imbalance in tumour regions, and the need for robust intensity normalisation.
Our work addresses these challenges by incorporating channel‑wise attention and lightweight
residual blocks.

RELATED WORK
UNet derivatives, V‑Net, HighRes‑Net and, more recently, transformer hybrids have been
explored for brain MRI.  Attention mechanisms, first introduced by Oktay et al., improve
boundary delineation by gating irrelevant activations.  However, most implementations are
2‑D, limiting volumetric context.  Concurrently, efficiency studies emphasise reduced
parameter count for real‑time clinical deployment.

METHODS
Network Architecture  We embed channel‑wise attention gates after each encoder block.
The backbone uses depthwise separable 3‑D convolutions, trimming parameters by 38 %
compared with a vanilla 3‑D UNet.  The resulting model has 7.2 M parameters.

Preprocessing Pipeline  Following recommendations from Li et al. [3], we apply Z‑score
intensity normalisation and bias‑field correction.  All volumes are resampled to 1 mm³
isotropic resolution and skull‑stripped with HD‑BET.

Training Strategy  The dataset is split 80 / 20 (train/validation) in accordance with the
evaluation protocol proposed in [3].  Class imbalance is mitigated via a weighted Dice +
Cross‑Entropy composite loss (α = 0.7).  Training uses a batch size of 2, Dice loss,
learning rate 0.001, Adam optimiser, and runs for 200 epochs.

RESULTS
Quantitative Evaluation  On the BraTS 2020 test set, our model achieves:
  • Whole‑tumour Dice  0.892  
  • Tumour‑core Dice   0.873  
  • Enhancing‑tumour   0.854  
These scores exceed the best 2‑D hybrid UNet by 0.03–0.05 absolute Dice.

Boundary Delineation  The attention gate improves tumour margin precision, reducing
Hausdorff 95% distance by 12 %.  Visual inspection confirms fewer false positives in
healthy tissue.

Efficiency  Parameter count drops from 11.5 M (baseline 3‑D UNet) to 7.2 M, cutting
inference latency from 420 ms to 305 ms per volume on an RTX 2080 Ti.

DISCUSSION
Our Dice of 0.892 compares favourably to prior 3‑D CNNs that reported ≤ 0.86 under the
same protocol.  The volumetric approach preserves inter‑slice context, delivering superior
consistency across slices.  Nevertheless, memory constraints still limit batch size.
Future work may explore mixed‑precision training and patch‑wise inference for deployment
on resource‑constrained hardware.

CONCLUSION
Attention‑gated 3‑D UNet achieves state‑of‑the‑art accuracy while adding minimal
computational cost.  By integrating efficiency considerations and robust preprocessing,
the model is a practical candidate for clinical MRI workflows.
